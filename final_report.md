**Executive Summary**  
AI agents are defined by IBM and Google Cloud as autonomous software systems that perform tasks on behalf of users or other systems. Market research projects the AI‑agents sector to grow from USD 12.06 billion in 2026 to USD 53.2 billion by 2030 (44.9 % CAGR). Early adopters report that 80 % of organizations see measurable ROI, indicating tangible business value. These findings suggest strong growth potential but also highlight the need for careful governance and verification of claims.

**Key Findings**  
1. **Definition** – Both IBM and Google Cloud describe an AI agent as a software system that autonomously performs tasks for a user or another system.  
2. **Market Size Forecast** – The AI Agents Market is projected to rise from USD 12.06 billion in 2026 to USD 53.2 billion by 2030 (44.9 % CAGR).  
3. **Enterprise Adoption & ROI** – 80 % of organizations report measurable ROI from AI agents; agents have moved from experiment to production.  
4. **Additional Forecasts** – Gartner (2025‑08‑26) predicts 40 % of enterprise apps will include task‑specific AI agents by 2026. Gartner (2025‑06‑25) forecasts that >40 % of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end‑2027, underscoring adoption risks.

**Analysis and Recommendations**  
The shared definition provides a common framework for product labeling and regulatory compliance. The market forecast signals significant upside for vendors and investors, while the high ROI statistic supports broader deployment. Decision‑makers should pursue pilot projects with clear success metrics, establish governance to mitigate the projected cancellation risk, and seek independent validation of ROI claims before scaling.

**Limitations**  
- Source pages were not directly accessed; all figures derive from search‑result snippets.  
- Publication dates for the ResearchAndMarkets, Anthropic, and G2 reports are not shown in the snippets.  
- The 80 % ROI figure is self‑reported; methodology is undisclosed.  
- Gartner numbers are explicit forecasts, not measured outcomes.  
- No independent verification of the 2026‑dated primary sources beyond titles and URLs.
